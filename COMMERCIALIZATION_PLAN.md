# Commercialization Plan

## Initial customer

Small and midsize B2B suppliers that receive semi-structured RFQs, price from a controlled catalog, need margin approval and send formal quotes by email. Start with one vertical where quote volume and approval friction are observable; avoid regulated document categories until privacy/security obligations are reviewed.

## Validation sequence

1. Interview 15–20 sellers/managers about current RFQ volume, turnaround, margin leakage and approval paths.
2. Run five operator-assisted design-partner pilots with written success criteria.
3. Measure factual activation and repeated weekly use; collect no fabricated testimonial or ROI.
4. Convert through Stripe test/live only after billing, support and data policies pass staging.

## Packaging hypothesis

Free, Pro and Business are configurable entitlement labels. Stripe Price IDs determine actual amounts. Test willingness to pay using customer interviews and paid pilots before changing limits. Keep the core quote workflow intact across plans; charge for scale (team, volume, AI/storage) and administration rather than withholding essential financial correctness.

## Metrics

Activation: configured organization, catalog/customer added, first RFQ and sent quote. Engagement: active organizations, RFQs/quotes/sends/responses per week. Revenue: paying organizations, subscription mix and Stripe-derived MRR. Quality: send failures, time to quote, approval delays, response conversion and support burden. Retention and cohort claims begin only after adequate elapsed time.

## Go-to-market experiments

Founder-led outbound to regional distributors/service suppliers; workflow audit plus pilot; channel conversations with fractional sales operations consultants; focused content about RFQ turnaround and margin control. Stop experiments that do not yield qualified discovery or repeated product use.
